01The threat

The weather swings hardest where the farms are smallest.

Taiwan has warmed 0.29 °C per decade since 1991, nearly three times the earlier rate. We scored every county for how far its temperature and rain swing, then laid every farm parcel on the main island over the result.

No instruments on a small farm

Before a dry spell, Ms. Chen lays dried weeds over her soil so the morning dew soaks in slowly. That buys her about three weeks before she checks again, a rhythm learned over twenty-five years on one plot. She reads stress by eye.

Flash droughts now form faster, and nearly half develop inside five days. One slow week in a three-week rhythm is enough to lose the crop.

02The gap

Protectants are made far from the farm. Making them nearby brings live bacteria onto it.

Biological protectants that carry a plant through heat and drought are on sale in Taiwan today. A grower buys them from a company that owns a fermenter, a cold chain and a registration dossier, then waits for the delivery.

So why not buy them?Because the bottle is made for an average season.

Dried spore products keep at room temperature and cost little. Timing and dose are what fail. A product is made months before anyone knows what the weather will do, packed in fixed sizes for an average field, and delivered on the distributor’s schedule. A drought that forms in five days arrives before the order does.

The catch

Making it on the farm puts live engineered bacteria on the farm.

The protein a plant needs is made by engineered cells, and a fermenter moved to the field brings those cells with it. The protein has to reach the soil. The cells that make it must stay out, on land that was never built to hold them.

03The distance

The newest protectants spend years in a lab before a farmer can buy one.

What reaches a farm shop finished its testing long ago. The protectants being discovered now sit in journals and university labs, far from the people who would use them.
